School Clubs & Movements

Drama Club

Telling stories through voice, movement and imagination.

Drama is the art of telling a story by acting it out. In the Drama Club, pupils learn to use their voices, faces, bodies and imaginations to bring characters and stories to life. The club builds creative performance skills, confidence on stage and the ability to express ideas, and it gives every member a chance to shine, whether on the stage or behind it.

Drama is more than entertainment. Through acting, pupils learn to understand other people’s feelings, to work in a team and to share messages that matter, such as honesty, hygiene, respect and care for the environment.

At a glance

What it is
Acting out stories, plays, skits and poems
Roles
Actors, director, prompter, stage manager, props and costume helpers
Materials
Simple props and costumes made from local and recycled materials
Performs at
School events, assemblies and Music, Dance and Drama (MDD) festivals
Key skills
Voice, movement, memory, imagination and teamwork

What the club aims to do

  • Build confidence in standing and speaking before an audience.
  • Develop imagination, creativity and storytelling.
  • Teach clear speaking, expressive movement and good memory.
  • Use drama to share positive messages with the school and the community.
  • Teach teamwork, patience and respect for others.

Drama words explained

Script
The written words of a play, including what each character says and does.
Cast
The group of actors who perform in a play.
Character
The person or animal an actor pretends to be.
Dialogue
A conversation between two or more characters.
Mime
Acting without words, using only the face and the body.
Improvisation
Acting without a script, making up the words and actions on the spot.
Props
Objects that actors use on stage, such as a bag, a stick or a book.
Cue
A line or action that tells an actor when to speak or move.
Stage directions
Instructions about where to stand and move, such as “stage left” and “stage right”, which are the actor’s left and right when facing the audience.
Rehearsal
Practice before the real performance.

Training and warm-ups

  • Voice: speaking loudly and clearly without shouting, speaking at the right speed and using a voice that suits the character.
  • Body and movement: good posture, gestures and facial expressions that show feelings.
  • Mime games: activities such as “mirror”, where partners copy each other’s movements, and “freeze”, where actors stop in a still pose.
  • Improvisation games: making up short scenes from a word or a picture.
  • Memory and concentration: learning lines and listening carefully to other actors.

From idea to performance

  1. The group chooses a story or a theme, such as honesty or caring for the environment.
  2. The script is written or adapted with the help of the teacher in charge.
  3. Roles are given out. Everyone is given a part, onstage or backstage.
  4. The cast reads the script aloud together to understand the story.
  5. The director shows where actors will stand and move. This is called blocking.
  6. The cast rehearses many times, learning lines and improving expression.
  7. Props, costumes and simple scenery are prepared.
  8. A dress rehearsal is held, where the play is performed in full, as if for the audience.
  9. The performance takes place, followed by feedback and discussion.

Kinds of drama the club performs

  • Traditional folktales and stories that teach a lesson.
  • Plays on good behaviour, honesty, respect and obedience.
  • Drama with a message about health, hygiene, the environment, road safety and children’s rights.
  • Short comedy skits for school entertainment.
  • Choral verse, poems and storytelling.
  • Scenes from important events in history.

Backstage roles

Not everyone acts. Many roles are vital behind the stage. The director guides the whole group. The prompter follows the script and helps actors who forget lines. The stage manager makes sure props and people are ready at the right moment. Props and costume helpers collect, make and look after the things the actors use. Every role teaches responsibility, and every member is important.

Good manners on stage and in the audience

  • Arrive on time for rehearsals and know your lines.
  • Listen to the director and to other actors.
  • Never laugh at or mock a classmate who makes a mistake.
  • Stay in character until the scene is finished.
  • As an audience member, keep quiet, watch attentively and clap at the end.

Dealing with stage fright

Nearly every performer feels nervous before going on stage. Members learn to take slow, deep breaths, to know their lines well, to look at a spot at the back of the room, and to remember that the audience wants them to do well. Practice and friendly support make the fear smaller each time.

Skills and values pupils gain

  • Confidence and clear speech.
  • Creativity and imagination.
  • Memory and concentration.
  • Empathy, which means understanding how other people feel.
  • Teamwork, patience and responsibility.

Who can join, and how

Any interested pupil may join. No experience is needed. The teacher in charge matches members with roles that suit them, and members try different roles over time.

For meeting days, times and any contributions, please ask the teacher in charge or the school office.

Frequently asked questions

My child is shy. Is drama suitable?

Yes. Drama helps shy children, because they begin with group games and small parts and move step by step towards bigger roles. Backstage roles are also available.

Do pupils have to memorise many lines?

Parts are matched to age and ability, and lines are learned over many rehearsals. Pupils also learn improvisation and mime, which need no memorising.

How are the stories chosen?

The teacher in charge chooses or approves the stories and plays so that they suit the age of the pupils and the values of the school.

Are costumes expensive?

No. Costumes and props are kept simple and are often made from materials found at school or at home. The teacher in charge explains anything that members need to bring.

Does the club take part in competitions?

The club prepares for school events and for Music, Dance and Drama (MDD) festivals when the school takes part.

Drama gives pupils a voice, an imagination and the courage to share both. On the stage and behind it, members learn to work together to tell a story well.
