School Clubs & Movements

Scripture Union

Growing in faith, fellowship and good character.

Scripture Union (SU) is a Christian movement that helps children and young people to read the Bible, to pray and to live by Christian values. It began in England in the nineteenth century as a ministry to children and now works in many countries around the world, including Uganda, where SU supports groups in schools.

In our school, the Scripture Union club encourages spiritual growth, moral values, fellowship and faith-centred leadership. It gives pupils a place to learn about God, to pray together, to sing and to make friendships built on respect, honesty and love.

At a glance

Type
An interdenominational Christian fellowship
Membership
Voluntary
Main activities
Bible reading, prayer, songs, Bible quizzes, drama and service
Values taught
Honesty, love, forgiveness, respect, obedience, humility and kindness
Led by
The teacher in charge, sometimes with invited guest speakers

What the club aims to do

  • Help pupils to know and to read the Bible in a way that suits their age.
  • Encourage a habit of prayer and thankfulness.
  • Build good character and moral values.
  • Provide fellowship, which means friendship and support among members.
  • Encourage pupils to serve others and to lead by good example.

What members do

Bible reading and reflection

  • Reading short Bible passages that are suitable for children, and learning what they mean.
  • Discussing questions in small groups, such as “What does this story teach us?”
  • Memorising short Bible verses.
  • Applying the lesson to everyday life, such as honesty in class, kindness at home and forgiveness among friends.

Prayer and worship

  • Opening and closing every meeting with prayer.
  • Praying for the school, families, the sick and the country.
  • Singing songs and choruses of praise.

Fellowship activities

  • Bible quizzes and competitions.
  • Acting out Bible stories in short dramas.
  • Reciting poems and sharing short messages prepared by members.
  • Special programmes such as Christmas and Easter celebrations.
  • Fellowship days with other groups, where these can be arranged.

Service to others

  • Sharing with pupils and families in need.
  • Visiting and encouraging the sick.
  • Helping to keep the school clean and tidy.
  • Welcoming new pupils and supporting classmates who are troubled.

A typical fellowship meeting

  1. Members gather and the leader opens with a welcome and a prayer.
  2. The group sings one or two songs together.
  3. A Bible passage is read aloud.
  4. The leader or a member shares a short message or a story that explains the passage.
  5. Members discuss questions in small groups.
  6. There is a time of prayer, when members may pray aloud or silently.
  7. Announcements are given, and the meeting closes with a final prayer or song.

Values the club teaches

Honesty
Telling the truth and not cheating or stealing.
Love and kindness
Caring about other people and helping them.
Forgiveness
Letting go of anger and starting again with those who wrong us.
Respect
Treating teachers, parents, classmates and people of all backgrounds with honour.
Obedience
Listening to parents and teachers and following good rules.
Humility
Not boasting, and being ready to learn and to serve.

Skills and values pupils gain

  • A habit of reading and reflection.
  • Good moral values and self-discipline.
  • Speaking and leadership skills through leading prayers, songs and readings.
  • Friendship, encouragement and a sense of belonging.
  • A heart for service and for peace.

Who can join, and how

Membership is voluntary. Pupils join because they want to, and parents or guardians are free to guide their children’s participation. Pupils from different Christian traditions fellowship together, and the club teaches respect for everyone. A pupil who wishes to join can speak to the teacher in charge.

For meeting days, times and any contributions, please ask the teacher in charge or the school office.

Safety and discipline

Meetings take place on the school premises under the supervision of the teacher in charge. Any guest speakers are invited by the school. Members are expected to behave respectfully, to take turns to speak and to listen to one another. The club is a place where every pupil should feel welcome and safe.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scripture Union only for pupils from one church?

No. It is interdenominational, which means that it brings together Christians from different churches.

Must my child join?

No. Membership is voluntary.

Does the club replace church or religious lessons?

No. It supports what pupils learn at home, at church and in class, but it does not replace them.

What should my child bring?

A notebook and a pen are usually enough. Pupils who have a Bible are encouraged to bring it. The teacher in charge explains any other needs.

How does the club help in school life?

Honesty, respect, obedience, forgiveness and self-discipline make pupils better members of the class and the school.

Scripture Union gives pupils a firm foundation of faith, love and good character, which helps them to make wise choices throughout their lives.
